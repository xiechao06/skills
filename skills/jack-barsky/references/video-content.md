## Biography & Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 1949 | Born **Albrecht Dittrich** in remote southeast East Germany, four years and ten days after Germany's unconditional surrender in WWII. Genetically half-German, half Czech/Polish. |
| Late 1960s | Exceptional chemistry student at University of Jena. Wins the **Karl Marx Scholarship** (limited to 100 concurrent recipients in the entire country). Works as assistant professor. |
| ~1969 | A stranger knocks on his dormitory door on a Saturday, pretending to be from Carl Zeiss Jena (optics company). Barsky sees through the ruse; guesses Stasi. |
| ~1969–1970 | Introduced to "Hermann," a KGB officer. Informal 18-month evaluation period: in-depth conversations, hitchhiking trip report, profiles of fellow students, doorbell tests on strangers. |
| ~1970 | Practice trips to West Berlin: first to "smell the air," second with a cover-story task. |
| ~1970 | Official invitation meeting with KGB chief in East Berlin. Told: *"Are you in or not? You have until tomorrow."* Decides to join (adventure over tenure). Gives up his Communist Party card. |
| ~1970–1972 | Two-year **training in Berlin** under handler "Nikolai": Morse code, shortwave radio, one-time-pad encryption, secret writing, microdots, surveillance detection routes, English (100 words/day via index cards). Also studies West German culture, watches West German TV. |
| ~1972–1978 | Two-year **training in Moscow**: American-English phonetics with a US-born tutor, operational drills, SDR training, learns to drive. Lives in near-total social isolation. |
| ~1977 | Three-month **practice trip to Canada** (as "Henry Van Randall"). Obtains a birth certificate of a deceased person; escapes RCMP surveillance. |
| 1978 | Enters US on a **Canadian passport as William Dyson**, deplaning in Chicago (unplanned). Stays one night on Chicago's South Side; tries to burn passport, cuts it up and flushes it. Moves to New York. |
| 1978–1979 | First year in NYC: acquires Social Security card, driver's license. Works as a **bike messenger** — a low-profile role that lets him listen and observe. |
| Early 1980s | Becomes a **computer programmer**; rises to management level. First personal computer ~1984; writes his own spaced-repetition flashcard program. |
| 1983 | Tasked with surveillance of **Earl Reppin naval station** in New Jersey (Operation RYAN — Soviet fear of US launching nuclear first strike under Reagan). |
| Late 1980s | Married, has daughter **Chelsea** (18 months old when danger signal arrives). Living the American dream — close to a McMansion. |
| December 1988 | Sees **red danger signal** (red dot on subway support beam in Queens) — protocol demands immediate flight from the country. Goes to work instead; stares at computer screen all day. |
| Late 1988 | Shortwave message: *"We have reason to believe the FBI is on your case. Execute emergency procedure. Come home right away."* |
| Late 1988 | KGB officer approaches him at Queens subway station and whispers: **"You gotta come back or else you're dead."** |
| Late 1988 | Goes to **Staten Island dead drop** to pick up money and passport. Container is not there. Walking away, says to himself: *"I'm staying."* |
| Late 1988 | Writes final secret-writing letter to KGB: claims he has contracted **HIV/AIDS** (unrefusable excuse in that era). KGB believes it; tells his German family he is dead. |
| Late 1988 | Throws shortwave radio into the **Hudson River**; destroys one-time pads. |
| ~1989–1993 | Moves to rural **Pennsylvania**, buys a house, has second child. Builds quiet American life. |
| ~1992 | **Vasily Mitrokhin**, KGB archivist, defects to MI6 bringing notes smuggled in underwear over years. Notes include: *"Jack Barsky, illegal, northeast USA."* MI6 shares with FBI. |
| ~1994 | FBI identifies Jack Barsky via Social Security (card obtained age 33 — impossible for a genuine American). Begins three-year surveillance. **Buys the house next door** to Barsky in PA. Plants listening device in his kitchen. |
| ~1996 | During an argument, Barsky confesses to wife what he sacrificed for her and Chelsea — unknowingly **recorded by the kitchen bug**. FBI now knows he is inactive and cooperative. |
| **1997** | FBI stops Barsky at a **Hudson River toll booth**. First words: *"Am I under arrest?"* Then: *"What took you so long?"* Immediately offers full cooperation. His FBI interviewer, **Joe Riley**, later becomes godfather to his last child. |
| 1997–~2014 | Years of cooperation with FBI; lives under cover while citizenship paperwork processes. Birth year changed (from 1944 to avoid early Social Security fraud). |
| **~2015** | Sworn in as **US citizen** ("seven years ago" from the 2022 podcast date). |
| 2015–2016 | Story goes public: relative's connection → *Der Spiegel* reporter → **Steve Kroft** at *60 Minutes*. |
| 2017 | Publishes **"Deep Undercover: My Secret Life and Tangled Allegiances as a KGB Spy in America."** |

## KGB Structure & Successor Agencies

- **KGB** (Komitet Gosudarstvennoy Bezopasnosti = Committee for State Security): founded 1954, dissolved 1991.
- **FSB**: domestic security/counterintelligence (≈ FBI), but has taken on espionage too.
- **SVR**: foreign intelligence (≈ CIA), relatively weak.
- **GRU**: military intelligence — Barsky says it is the *most capable* Russian agency today; nobody knows much about it.
- Predecessor chain: Cheka → GPU → NKVD → KGB.
- Of 8 KGB heads between 1923–1953, **6 were executed** when replaced.
- The USSR was the only dictatorship that rested power on the intelligence apparatus rather than the military — inherently unstable.

## Tradecraft: Key Terms & Techniques

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| **Illegal** | Spy with no diplomatic cover; if caught, their country denies all knowledge. |
| **Dead drop** | Pre-agreed hidden location to exchange items without meeting. |
| **Handler** | KGB officer who manages and directs an agent. |
| **Compartmentalization** | Each person knows only what they need to; cells of 3–4 people. |
| **One-time pad** | Unbreakable encryption: sender and receiver each hold identical random key sheets used only once. Barsky's pad required iodine to develop. |
| **Shortwave radio / Numbers station** | KGB broadcast encrypted digit-groups on shortwave. Barsky would write them down and manually decrypt with his algorithm. |
| **Secret writing** | His notepad's first 10 pages were impregnated with a trace chemical — invisible ink for writing messages that could be mailed in an innocuous letter. |
| **Microdot** | Photograph reduced with a reversed microscope to the size of a pinhead; hidden under a postage stamp. Barsky was trained but never used it operationally. |
| **SDR (Surveillance Detection Route)** | A 3-hour preplanned route through a city, using natural behavior (phone booths, elevators, public transport) to detect tails. Required monthly in the US. |
| **Counter-surveillance** | The art of detecting that you are being followed. Barsky had an excellent memory for faces — he beat every KGB training exercise. |
| **Cover story / Legend** | A false but plausible biography and identity. Barsky's legend: "Jack Barsky," a genuine American citizen. |
| **Signal spot** | A visible location (lamppost, wall) where a mark (red dot, chalk line) indicates a message or danger. |
| **Conspiratorial flat** | An apartment used for secret meetings; the occupant leaves when agents arrive. |
| **Spotter** | An agent who identifies and profiles potential recruits or persons of interest. Barsky's primary US function. |

## Historical & Ideological Context

### Why Barsky believed in communism
1. Communists were the only force that physically fought the Nazis before Hitler took power.
2. As a 17-year-old top student he was required to tour a concentration camp — the horror confirmed the Nazis were evil and the communists the heroes.
3. Ex-Nazis held prominent positions in West Germany (e.g., Reinhard Gehlen ran the BND / West German CIA after being co-opted by the CIA).
4. The world map was slowly turning "pink" (communist-friendly) in the Third World.

### Why the Soviet system failed (Barsky's view)
- Every hierarchy goes bad when the wrong people rise to the top.
- Lenin's "Red Terror" started at the Soviet Union's birth — terror was built in from day one.
- Paranoia at the top flows down; truth never reaches leaders.
- Stalin's purge of capable generals cost dearly in WWII.
- The underground economy and endemic theft/corruption revealed systemic rot.

### On Vladimir Putin
- Mediocre KGB agent: deployed to **East Germany** (not West Germany, Switzerland, or Austria where the best went).
- Excellent politician and organizer — cleaned up Yeltsin's chaotic Russia.
- Witnessed KGB's humiliation in Dresden when the Wall fell; Gorbachev's order "do not use force" left Putin powerless.
- Barsky believes Putin became obsessed with restoring Russian greatness from that moment.
- "Plausible deniability": Putin likely does not give direct assassination orders; he "muses" and subordinates act.

### On the Ukraine War (2022)
- Barsky says "savvy observers saw this coming" — Putin was openly stating his goals for years.
- American "wishful thinking" (Obama's reset, failure to sanction after Crimea) enabled escalation.
- NATO expansion and arms supply to Ukraine also bear responsibility — the "rat" pushed into a corner.
- He calls it the most dangerous crisis of his lifetime because of the nuclear dimension.
- A protracted conflict increases accident risk exponentially.

## Themes

| Theme | Key moments |
|-------|-------------|
| **Love as the decisive force** | First girlfriend inspired academic excellence. Love for daughter Chelsea made him refuse the recall. Final lesson: "Love conquers all." |
| **Identity & reinvention** | Albrecht → Dita → Jack Barsky → American citizen. The American identity gradually became real. |
| **Competence vs. incompetence** | KGB was superb at compartmentalization but totally incompetent at Americanization — they had no idea what daily American life was like. |
| **Trust & betrayal** | His entire life was lies, yet the KGB relationship was built on trust. He betrayed the KGB but did so for love. |
| **Adventure vs. stability** | The James Bond fantasy vs. a comfortable tenured professorship. Adventure won in youth; love and family won in middle age. |
| **Paranoia & power** | Stalin's paranoia destroyed the KGB from within. Same pattern visible in Putin's Russia today. |
